Brownfield vs Greenfield vs Selective Data Transition: Choosing Your S/4HANA Approach

The three ways to get to S/4HANA compared side by side, with the questions that decide which one is right for you.

By the Oddesy Consulting and Services team · 7 Nov 2026

Every move from SAP ECC to SAP S/4HANA starts with one big decision: do you convert your existing system, start again, or do something in between? The choice affects your budget, timeline, risk and how much your processes improve. This guide compares the three main approaches.

The three approaches at a glance

Brownfield (system conversion)Greenfield (new implementation)Selective data transition
What it isConvert your existing ECC system to S/4HANABuild a new S/4HANA system and migrate selected dataBuild a new S/4HANA shell and move selected configuration and data
Historical dataKept in fullUsually open items and balances onlyChosen by you (by company, year or object)
Process redesignLimited, can be done laterFull redesign on SAP best practicePartial, where it adds value
Typical effortLowestHighestMedium to high
Best forStable, well-run processesOutdated processes, heavy customisationMergers, carve-outs, multi-system consolidation

Brownfield: system conversion

A brownfield conversion takes your current ECC system, including configuration, custom code and history, and technically converts it to S/4HANA using SAP's Software Update Manager and related tools.

Advantages

  • Fastest route with the least business disruption.
  • All historical data stays available.
  • Users keep familiar processes, reducing training effort.

Watch-outs

  • You carry existing custom code and workarounds into the new system.
  • Simplification items (for example, Business Partner replacing customer and vendor master) still require work.
  • Process improvement is postponed rather than avoided.

Greenfield: new implementation

Greenfield means implementing S/4HANA fresh, usually based on SAP best practices and fit-to-standard workshops, then migrating master data and open balances.

Advantages

  • A clean core with minimal custom code, which makes future upgrades easier.
  • An opportunity to standardise and simplify processes.
  • The natural fit for S/4HANA Cloud public edition.

Watch-outs

  • Longer and more expensive, with more change management.
  • Historical data needs a separate archive or reporting solution.

Selective data transition: the middle path

Selective data transition (sometimes called "bluefield" or hybrid) builds a new S/4HANA system and moves only what you choose, such as certain company codes, years of history, or reusable configuration. It typically uses specialist tools and partners.

It is especially useful when you want to merge several ECC systems into one, separate a business unit, or redesign some processes while keeping history for others.

Five questions that decide your approach

  1. How well do your current processes serve the business? If well, brownfield is attractive. If not, lean greenfield.
  2. How much custom code do you run, and how much is actually used? Usage analysis often shows a large share is unused.
  3. How important is historical data in the live system?
  4. Are you planning organisational changes such as acquisitions, divestments or consolidation?
  5. Which deployment model are you targeting? Public cloud generally means greenfield.

Our recommendation

Do not choose on instinct. A short readiness assessment, covering custom code usage, simplification items, data volumes and business priorities, will usually make the right answer clear and give you a defensible business case.

How a brownfield conversion works, step by step

  1. Preparation: check system requirements, run SAP Readiness Check, review simplification items and plan any required add-on updates.
  2. Pre-checks and preparatory work: convert customers and vendors to Business Partners using Customer-Vendor Integration, reconcile financial data and clean up inconsistencies.
  3. Custom code adaptation: analyse custom ABAP with the ABAP Test Cockpit and adapt code affected by data model changes.
  4. Technical conversion: use the Software Update Manager (SUM), often with Database Migration Option if you are moving from a non-HANA database.
  5. Application data migration: run the finance and logistics migration steps that move data into the new structures, such as the Universal Journal.
  6. Testing and cut-over: regression testing, user acceptance testing, then the production conversion over a planned downtime window.

What drives cost and timeline

Whichever approach you choose, the same factors usually decide how long the project takes and what it costs:

  • Custom code volume and how much of it is actually used.
  • Data volume and quality, especially in finance and material master data.
  • Number of interfaces to banks, warehouses, e-commerce and other systems.
  • Organisational scope: countries, company codes, plants and languages.
  • Appetite for process change and the strength of business ownership.

As a broad guide, a well-prepared brownfield conversion for a mid-sized company often runs 6 to 12 months, while greenfield programmes of similar scope commonly take 9 to 18 months. Your own figures depend heavily on the factors above.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing greenfield for a "fresh start" without the budget or business capacity for full process redesign.
  • Choosing brownfield and assuming no business involvement is needed. Simplification items still change how people work.
  • Leaving interface testing until the end of the project.
  • Not planning how historical data will be reported on after a greenfield move.

Frequently asked questions

What is a brownfield S/4HANA migration?

A brownfield migration, or system conversion, technically converts your existing SAP ECC system to S/4HANA, keeping configuration, custom code and historical data.

What is a greenfield S/4HANA implementation?

Greenfield means implementing a new S/4HANA system from scratch, usually on SAP best practices, and migrating only master data and open balances.

Which S/4HANA migration approach is cheapest?

Brownfield is usually the lowest-effort route, but total cost depends on custom code, data volumes and how much process change you need.
